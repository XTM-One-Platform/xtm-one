# Templates

A template is a **design your presentations wear**: a palette per theme, the heading and body typefaces, the logos drawn on every slide, the predefined assets a deck may place (figures, screenshots, badges, partner / integration / customer logos), the layouts it may use, and a few sentences of guidance on how to compose in that brand. Agents find templates by name, kind, aspect ratio, tag and by the themes they support, so the same request produces on-brand output for everyone. The page lists, for each template, the themes it supports — `Dark`, `Light` or both.

A template has one of three **kinds**, chosen when it is created: `Presentation` dresses slide decks and pages, `Report` dresses [executive reports](../reports.md), `Video` dresses the videos of the [Video Studio](../video-studio.md) (see [Video templates](#video-templates)). Whatever its kind, its assets are readable by any deck — there is no separate "asset pack" kind, the assets belong to the template whose brand they serve.

Templates live under `Agent Studio > Templates` in the sidebar. The page opens with the three steps of using one; they are spelled out below.

## Use a template

**1. Design it.** A template **declares the themes it supports** — `Dark`, `Light` or both — with two switches, when it is created and at any time on the `Configuration` tab (`Themes this template supports`); at least one stays on. Each supported theme gets **its own palette**: the **base palette** carries the full set of colours and the typefaces and is read as `Dark` or `Light` from its background colour, and the other supported theme gets a **variant palette** in which only the colours you set differ — the accent, fonts and logos carry over. A template that supports a single theme limits every deck wearing it to that theme; switching a theme off removes the choice from those decks (a deck already in that theme is snapped to a supported one at its next save, and the note says so). The template's `Preview` renders a sample deck in each supported theme so the palettes can be judged before any deck exists.

**2. Choose it when you create a presentation.** `New presentation`, on the Presentations page or on a template's own page (`New presentation with this template`), asks which template the deck starts in and which of its supported themes to use, then opens the chat with the request written for you. In a chat, when you can see several templates, the template is **your** choice, never the assistant's: an assistant asked for a presentation without a template named **lists your templates and asks which one to use** before creating anything (it may recommend one, but a new deck is never created in a template you did not pick); name it ("in the Sales template") and it does not ask, ask for "no template" and the deck is drawn in the plain built-in look. Automations, and chats where the assistant cannot ask, use the platform's **default presentation template** — the [built-in brand deck](#the-built-in-brand-deck) (the `XTM One Brand Deck` on an XTM One platform, the `Filigran Brand Deck` on Filigran Copilot) unless an administrator chose another company template in `Settings > Parameters`.

**3. Change it anytime.** A deck stays **linked** to its template rather than copying it: the palette, fonts and logos are read from the template every time the deck is drawn, so editing the template restyles every deck wearing it, at once. The deck's own `Design` section (`Edit slides` in the viewer) applies another template, or none, with a live preview, and offers the `Dark` / `Light` theme among the ones the chosen template supports. The slides, their words, the images uploaded to the deck and the theme it picked belong to the deck; deleting a template leaves its decks intact, in the plain look.

A template can also fix the **deck defaults** of new decks made from it, so nobody has to ask for the brand look each time — the theme (a default theme the template does not support is snapped to one it does), the 16:9 or 4:3 aspect ratio, the slide transition, and the **covers**: the hero backdrop, its colour and the theme of the title and closing slides, so a brand deck opens and closes on a dark gradient cover whatever the body's theme (the `Filigran Brand Deck` and the `XTM One Brand Deck` do: their decks open and close on their gradient cover). Every deck offers the `Plain`, `Gradient`, `Split` and `Spotlight` covers; the tiled ones — `Mosaic`, `Cascade`, `Horizon` and `Pillar` — are a brand's own artwork, offered only by a template that lists them under `heroes` in its `Extra metadata` (`"heroes": ["mosaic", "cascade", "horizon", "pillar"]`, as the `THREAD Conference Deck` does). A cover default must be one of the covers the template offers. Deck defaults live in the template's `Extra metadata` and in the Design editor, and they dress new decks only — an existing deck is never restyled by them. A template can define the [header and footer](#header-and-footer) every deck wearing it fills once, and it carries the **agent guidance** every assistant reads before composing a deck in that brand: which slide opens, where the numbers go, how sparingly to use the accent color. The `logo_mark` asset — the symbol without the wordmark — is what the gradient cover draws as a faint watermark in its corner.

A template also decides **how long a deck may run**. A deck holds up to 100 slides unless its template says otherwise: `max_slides` in the template's `Extra metadata` raises the limit for the kind of deck the template is for — a board pack or a quarterly review that gives every dense dashboard its own slide outgrows a pitch deck's budget — up to 300, the platform's ceiling. The `Filigran Brand Deck` ships with 150; the `XTM One Brand Deck` keeps the default. The limit applies when a deck grows: the assistant's `insert_slide` and the editor's `Add slide` and `Duplicate` stop at it and say which limit it is, a deck without a template keeps the default, and a deck that is already longer than its template now allows (the template was changed, or the deck was moved to a shorter one) stays editable as it is — it simply cannot take more slides until it is back under the limit. Exports and the viewer handle a long deck like any other.

Only the template's palette, fonts, logos and footer pattern reach a deck. A template can restyle a presentation, but it can never inject arbitrary styling or scripts into the pages that render it.

## Header and footer

The line at the bottom of every slide of a deck is not written on the slides — it is drawn from a few deck settings. A template decides **what that line says** for every deck wearing it, as a **pattern** with placeholders:

```
{{event}} - {{title}} - {{classification}}
```

- `{{title}}`, `{{subtitle}}` and `{{event}}` are always available: the deck's title, the subtitle of its opening slide (as plain text — inline emphasis such as `[[accent:...]]` is not drawn in a footer) and the event the deck names. They need no declaration.
- Every other placeholder is a **variable** the template declares — a key (`classification`), the label the deck's settings show (`Classification`), an optional hint and an optional default. A deck fills its variables once, in its `Header & footer` settings; until it does, a variable reads the template's default, or nothing when the template declares none.
- An empty placeholder disappears **with its separator**: a deck with no event reads `Quarterly review - Confidential`, not `- Quarterly review - Confidential`. Everything that is not a placeholder is drawn as written.
- A **header** pattern is optional and follows the same rules; it is drawn along the top of the same slides as the footer, and hides with it.

The `XTM One Brand Deck` defines `{{event}} - {{title}} - {{classification}}`. The event and the classification are both optional and have no default, so a new deck reads its own title on every slide, and "mark it Confidential" is one field, not a slide-by-slide edit.

On the `Configuration` tab, the Design editor's `Header & footer` card holds the footer pattern, the optional header pattern and the variables (up to eight), and previews the line with sample values as you type. The declaration is **checked before it is saved** — a placeholder no variable declares, a variable no pattern uses, a key that is not a lowercase word, a missing label — and a declaration with a problem is refused rather than drawn literally on every slide. `Overview` shows the pattern and the variables of a template that has them. A template with **no pattern** leaves the footer to each deck, which writes an event and a free footer text in the same settings. Changing the pattern restyles every deck wearing the template, like the palette; a field a deck set for a former pattern is kept, and the deck's settings say when no footer reads it.

The pattern is part of the design an assistant authoring a template can set (`chrome` in the template's metadata, with `footer`, `header` and `variables`), and `get_template` shows it to any assistant composing a deck, which is how "set the event to FIC 2026" reaches the right field.

### Brand watermark

The same card has a **Brand watermark** switch. On, every content, agenda, roadmap, takeaways and dashboard slide of a deck wearing the template carries the template's mark (its `logo_mark` asset) faint in the top-right corner, over a soft glow of the brand gradient rising from the bottom corners — the cover's watermark, quietened for slides that have work to show. Title, section and closing slides keep their own backdrops. The mark is inked for the slide's theme, so it reads on a light slide as well as on a dark one. A template with no `logo_mark` asset draws nothing, and a deck that hides its logo hides the watermark with it. In the PowerPoint export the watermark is one picture under each body slide, behind blocks that all stay editable. An assistant sets it with `"watermark": true` in the template's `chrome`. The `Filigran Brand Deck` has it off: following the Brand team's flat design rules, its body slides carry the corner emblem instead, and its footer reads the deck title, the event and the classification separated by middle dots (`{{title}} · {{event}} · {{classification}}`), each part optional. Its colours are the exact brand values: a flat `#0A0C1C` page with a Sky Blue accent in the `Dark` theme, an ice blue `#E6F7FF` page with a Royal Blue accent in the `Light` theme, chart series in brand colours with no grey.

### Corner emblem

The **Corner emblem** switch, next to it, draws the template's mark small and in full colour in the top-right corner of every slide except the title and closing ones, which keep their cover. Unlike the watermark it also appears on section slides, so the brand sign stays in view through the whole talk. A template with no `logo_mark` asset draws nothing, and a deck that hides its logo hides the emblem with it. In the PowerPoint export the emblem is a picture of its own, like the logo. An assistant sets it with `"emblem": true` in the template's `chrome`. The `THREAD Conference Deck` has it on.

## The built-in brand deck

Every platform ships the `XTM One Brand Deck`, a company-managed template: the product's own purple palette on dark surfaces (it supports the `Dark` theme only), Geologica and IBM Plex typefaces, six ordered colours for the series of a chart, four brand colours (the XTM One, OpenCTI, OpenAEV and OpenCRQ product colours, which the boxes of an architecture diagram can wear), the XTM One logo and mark, and the product material of the XTM platform as predefined assets: the OpenCTI, OpenAEV, OpenCRQ, XTM Hub, XTM One and XTM Platform logos and marks, the XTM platform architecture and product-flow diagrams, and product screenshots. It is the working example of a template every platform has, and the default presentation template of an XTM One platform; Filigran Copilot, Filigran's internal edition, also ships the `Filigran Brand Deck` and defaults to it.

Filigran Copilot also ships the `THREAD Conference Deck`, for the keynotes of THREAD, Filigran's conference: the colours of the THREAD design kit's color set on near-black slides (Cyan for highlights, the Lime-to-Blue gradient), and a `Light` theme drawn in the same set on Alabaster (the logo in dark ink, Cobalt Blue for highlights and the gradient's start, the mosaic tiles in their own colours); Elms Sans headings; title and closing slides on the `Cascade` cover of the THREAD marketing kit (the logo large in the top-left corner, tiles stepping down from the top-right one, the headline low over a thin rule and the speakers and date row), with `Mosaic`, `Horizon` and `Pillar` one click away in the slide's `Selection` tab - these four tiled covers are THREAD's own, offered by no other template; the "thread, powered by Filigran" logo in the corner and the mosaic "t" as the [corner emblem](#corner-emblem). Its footer signs every slide with the edition, the talk's title and its classification (`THREAD 2026 - The Road Ahead - Public`): the edition reads `THREAD 2026` until a deck sets another, and the classification is one field of the deck's `Header & footer` settings. It carries the same Filigran product, partner, integration and customer logos as the `Filigran Brand Deck`, plus the THREAD mosaic artworks, and its assistant guidance is written for a keynote: the session type above the title, every speaker and the date and room on the cover, numbered section dividers, few blocks per slide, and a closing slide with the speaker's contacts.

The logo is drawn automatically on every slide of a deck made from it. To give an assistant your own figures to place — your architecture diagram, your screenshots, your partners' logos — duplicate it or create a template of your own and add them on its `Assets` tab.

### Predefined assets

The assets of a template are what an assistant places when you ask for a figure, so it uses the actual one instead of drawing an approximation.

Every asset has a **description** that says what the figure shows and which surface (dark or light) it is drawn for; assistants read these descriptions to pick the right file, and you see them in the `Assets` tab. A template can hold hundreds of assets, so an assistant never reads the whole list: it asks the template for one **role** ("the partner logos") or one **keyword** and gets only what matches — ask for "a logo wall of our partners" and it lays the right logos out in a logo grid.

!!! note "Upgrading an XTM One platform"
    Earlier versions also shipped the `Filigran Brand Deck`, Filigran's own house style, on XTM One platforms. It is reserved to Filigran's internal edition: on the first start after the upgrade — whether AI is switched on or off — it is removed together with every one of its assets and their stored files, and whatever the object store could not delete at that start is deleted within the hour once it answers again. The decks made from it keep their slides and are drawn in the plain look until you choose another template in their `Design` section, such as the `XTM One Brand Deck`. A picture a slide had placed from it is removed with it and nothing is drawn in its place; editing that slide, asking an assistant to rewrite the deck or restoring an earlier revision still works — the picture stays gone and the history says how many pictures were left empty — and you can put another picture in its place at any time. If an administrator had chosen it as the `Default Presentation Template`, the setting returns to the built-in default. A copy you made with `Duplicate` is your own template and stays: delete it from the Templates page if it should go too.

!!! note "Upgrading from the asset libraries"
    Earlier versions shipped the partner, integration and customer logos as three separate `asset-library` templates next to the brand deck. On an XTM One platform the first start after the upgrade removes them, logos included. On Filigran Copilot, which ships the `Filigran Brand Deck`, it moves their logos into that deck instead — the same files under the same identifiers, so every slide that already placed one keeps it — and removes the three emptied libraries. A template of that former kind you created yourself keeps working with its assets, but no new one can be created: give the logos to a `Presentation` template instead. `Import` refuses a package of that kind and names it, so edit the kind in the export first; `Duplicate` copies such a template as a `Presentation` one, keeping the palette keys and layouts a presentation honours; whatever it had to leave out is listed in the audit log entry of the copy.

Built-in templates carry a `Built-in` badge and are **read-only**: they are kept in sync with the platform on every startup, so an edit would be undone by the next upgrade. To derive your own palette from one, use `Duplicate` — the copy is yours to change.

## Video templates

A `Video` template keeps every video that wears it to the same look. Its palette (per theme), fonts and logos work as they do for a deck - the `logo` and `logo_light` assets are drawn on the title and closing cards, the `logo_mark` asset is the watermark - and its rules live in `extra_metadata.video`:

- `aspect_ratios` — the formats the brand ships, the first one the default (`16:9`, `9:16`, `1:1`, `4:5`);
- `purposes` — what the template is made for: `product_demo`, `brand_film`, `event`, `launch`, `explainer`, `social` (`New video` suggests it for those kinds of video);
- `defaults` — what a video gets when it leaves it unset: `theme` (`mode`, `background` - `aurora`, `gradient`, `mesh`, `grid`, `mosaic` or `solid` - and `watermark`, the corner the mark is drawn in: a video writing `none` keeps none, and saving it says the template draws one), `default_transition` (`{"type": "auto", "pace": "calm"}` or `"dynamic"` to pick each cut from the scenes on either side, or one named `type` with an optional `duration`), `captions`, `voiceover` (`enabled`, `voice`, `style`, `speed`, and `pronunciations`: the brand's product names as the voice must say them), `music` (the brand's sound: a built-in `track` - `bright-horizon`, `deep-focus`, `signal`, `ascent`, `late-notes`, `momentum` or `quiet-light` - with its `volume`, `ducking`, `duck_db`, `fade_in` and `fade_out`; a video naming its own music keeps it, and `"music": {"enabled": false}` plays none);
- `scene_defaults` and `overlay_defaults` — the same per scene type (`title`, `statement`, `recording`, `slide`, `clip`, `image`, `outro`: the card style, how a statement is revealed and aligned and how dark its backdrop is, the browser window, the cursor, the camera) and per overlay type (`style`, `position`, `duration`, and a picture overlay's `width` and `opacity`). What a scene shows - its text, its lines, its recording, its backdrop, its narration - is never a template's to preset, with one exception: a scene type's defaults may add the brand's marks as picture overlays by their role, never a file - `"clip": {"overlays": [{"type": "image", "asset": "logo_mark", "position": "top_right", "width": 0.06, "duration": 60}]}` puts the template's mark on every AI shot and video clip, before the scene's own overlays, unless the scene draws that mark itself;
- `intro` and `outro` — `{"required": true, "scene": {...}}`: the title card added before the first scene and the closing card added after the last when the video has none (`{title}` in a text is the video's title); with `required: false` the card only lends its look to one the video writes;
- `locked` — the paths whose template value always wins, such as `theme.background`, `captions.style` or `scenes.recording.frame`, plus `theme.palette`, which keeps the template's colours and logo over any the video names;
- `shots` — the art direction of every AI shot made for a video wearing the template (see [AI shots wear the template's art direction](../video-studio.md#ai-shots-wear-the-templates-art-direction)): `style`, the look added to the prompt of a shot drawn from a description (up to 600 characters: the light, the lens, the materials, the palette in words, the motion); `avoid`, what no shot shows, one term each (up to 16: added to the negative prompt of a video model that takes one, and read on the finished shot's frames, which says when it shows one - name visual things, never text or a logo, which a shot made from a real screenshot carries). For example `"shots": {"style": "Photographic, natural light, deep navy and cyan tones.", "avoid": ["laser beams", "starfields", "holograms"]}`. It steers the shots, never limits how many a video has.

Every preset must make a valid video and every lock must name a preset: a rule that does not is refused when the template is saved, with the field named. The `Overview` tab shows the rules (`Video rules`), and `New video with this template` starts a video in it. Video templates ship with the platform for every kind of video: `Filigran Product Video`, `Filigran Brand Film`, `Filigran Event Loop` and `THREAD Conference Video` on Filigran Copilot, `XTM One Product Video`, `XTM One Brand Film`, `Threat Briefing` and `Social Teaser` on every platform (see [Video Studio templates](../video-studio.md#templates)). Every built-in video template carries the assets of its brand's deck: the four Filigran ones the whole library of the `Filigran Brand Deck` (product screenshots, diagrams, badges, team and event photos, and the product, partner, integration and customer logos), the THREAD one that of the `THREAD Conference Deck`, and the XTM One ones the product logos, diagrams and screenshots of the `XTM One Brand Deck`. The assistant places them as image scenes or statement backdrops, draws a screenshot onto a screen in a picture, or animates them as a figure - a wall of customer logos, an architecture whose boxes wear the product marks. The first product video a platform ships is the default of a video an assistant creates without naming a template, and `freestyle: true` opts a video out of any template (`New video` starts in free style unless a template is chosen or suggested for the kind of video).

## Create a template

From `Templates`, `Add Template` opens the creation form: a name, a description, the **kind** (`Presentation` for slide decks and pages, `Report` for executive reports, `Video` for Video Studio videos), the aspect ratio, tags, and — for administrators — whether the template is company-managed. A `Presentation` template also declares the **themes it supports** with two switches, `Dark` and `Light` — both on by default, at least one stays on. `Generate with AI` drafts the palette from a brief (see below) and proposes the themes its palettes read as, which you can still change; `Preview` renders a sample deck in it before you save.

Once the template exists, its detail page has three tabs:

- `Overview` — the description, the **Design** (the palette per theme, marked `Supported` or `Not supported`), the attributes agents search by (kind, aspect ratio, tags), the layout catalog, and how many times agents retrieved the template
- `Configuration` — the editable fields, the **Design editor** (`Themes this template supports`, one palette per supported theme with its colours — the base palette comes first and also carries the fonts — and the deck defaults), the layouts and the sharing settings; the raw JSON stays available under `Advanced (JSON)`. Every palette shows its **chart series** (`chart_1` to `chart_6`, drawn as a small bar preview that follows each keystroke; each theme tunes its own, and an empty series keeps the built-in colour, `chart_1` the accent) and its optional **brand hues** (`brand_1` to `brand_4`, the product colours an architecture node or zone can wear; an empty one falls back to the chart series of the same rank, and a variant palette inherits the base palette's). An empty field shows, dashed, the colour a deck draws instead: the colour it falls back to, or what a variant palette inherits from the base palette - its accent, gradient and brand hues, and every colour when the base palette's background is not set. When `Advanced (JSON)` also holds a variant for the base palette's own theme, which decks in that theme lay over the base, the form names the keys it sets, and editing one of them in the base palette removes it from that variant - except `background`, which decides the base palette's theme: remove a variant's `background` in `Advanced (JSON)`. `Preview the unsaved design` opens the template preview on everything being edited - the name, the aspect ratio, the layouts, the palettes and the deck defaults - so it shows what saving would produce; a name, an aspect ratio or layouts the save would refuse (a blank or over-long name, an over-long aspect ratio, layouts JSON that does not parse) are previewed as saved, and a palette value the save would refuse is left out of the preview.
- `Assets` — the files bound to the template

!!! note "The old theme label"
    Templates created before this version could carry a free-text theme label (`dark`, `corporate`, …). It never changed a deck's colours — only the palettes do — and is no longer part of the design. Where one is still set, `Configuration` shows it as `Search label (legacy)` with a `Clear` action; older agent instructions that looked templates up by that word keep working until it is cleared.

Templates are shared like every other resource: private to you, shared with groups, or company-managed for everyone. The `Export` action downloads the whole template as a package (metadata plus assets) that `Import` on the `Templates` page recreates on another platform.

## Generate a template with AI

Rather than typing color values by hand, let the assistant draft the template. `Generate with AI` is available on the `Templates` page, on the creation form, and on the `Configuration` tab of an existing template.

1. Write a **design brief** in plain language: the audience, the mood, two or three colors — "a light corporate deck for quarterly reviews, navy and gold, understated".
2. Add **palette hints** if you have them: a hex value or a color word, one chip each.
3. If the template already holds an image asset, pick it under `Sample colours from a logo`: the dominant colors of the image are handed to the assistant so the palette sits naturally around your logo.
4. Click `Generate`. A **live preview** of a sample deck in the proposed palette appears next to the result, together with the name, labels, layout catalog and agent guidance the assistant drafted, and the colors it read from the logo.
5. Refine in follow-up rounds — `Refine this result` with "warmer accent" or "swap the heading font for a geometric sans" — or `Regenerate from the brief` to start over.
6. `Apply to form` fills the fields. Nothing is saved until you save the template, and every field stays editable.

When you generate from the `Configuration` tab, `Start from the current configuration` keeps whatever the brief does not mention; turn it off for a fresh design. The proposal is checked against what the renderer supports: a value it would ignore is listed as an adjustment rather than silently dropped.

!!! tip "A logo first"
    To build a template around a logo, create the template, upload the logo on the `Assets` tab with the role `logo` (and the dark-on-light variant as `logo_light`), then run `Generate with AI` from `Configuration` with the logo selected. The logo is then drawn on every slide of the decks made from the template, and the palette is sampled from it.

## Assets

The `Assets` tab holds the files a template brings to a deck. Each asset has a **role** that tells the renderer and the agents what it is: `logo`, `logo_light`, `logo_mark`, `product_logo`, `partner_logo`, `integration_logo`, `customer_logo`, `people_photo`, `background`, `illustration`, `diagram`, `screenshot`, `badge`, `icon`, `image`, `font`, or `other` — and a free-text **description** that says what the file shows. A template holds up to 400 assets; the tab filters them by **role** (each role with its count) and by a **keyword** in the name or the description — the same two handles an assistant uses.

- The `logo` asset (and `logo_light` on the light theme) is drawn by the deck chrome on every slide.
- Other images are placed by the assistant when you ask for them — a product logo on the title slide, a background on a section divider, a diagram in an image block, several logos at once in a logo grid.
- The description is what an assistant picks an asset by ("the architecture diagram", "the dark-surface variant"), so write it for a reader who cannot see the file: what it shows, and whether it is drawn for a dark or a light surface.

You can add, relabel, replace and remove assets independently of the rest of the template. Replacing a file keeps its identity, so decks that already reference it pick up the new version. SVG files are cleaned on upload: scripts and remote references are removed before the file is stored.

## Ask an agent to author a template

The built-in `Template Designer` agent creates and refines templates from a conversation: describe the brand, answer its questions, and it creates the template, attaches the logos you uploaded to the chat, writes the deck defaults and the guidance, and generates a preview deck for you to judge. It cannot read colors out of an image — for that, use `Generate with AI` in the UI and bring the result back to the agent to refine.
