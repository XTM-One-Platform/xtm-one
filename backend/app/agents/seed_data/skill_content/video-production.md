# Video Production

How to turn a request for "a video" into a finished film with the Video Studio tools: a product demo, but just as often a brand film, an event loop, a launch teaser, a threat explainer or a social clip that mixes big statements, AI-generated shots, extracts of real footage, pictures and product recordings. The tool descriptions are the contract (every scene type and field, `create_video` / `edit_video`); this skill is the craft: the working method, the arc of each kind of video, how to write statements and AI shot prompts, how to use real footage, record the product and narrate a demo, what to do with what each save answers, how to wait for the long jobs, the review before rendering and the delivery.

**The person chooses; you make them aware.** The studio can do far more than one brief asks for. Your job is to show what it can do and let the person decide, never to impose a taste:
- **What the person or the "New video" dialog set wins** (its options arrive as lines of the first message: music, narration, captions, length, pace, format, template).
- **A choice left open is proposed, not taken silently.** Music, narration and voice, captions, length, pace, template or free style, AI shots vs recordings vs their own pictures, motion style: the storyboard (section 1, step 3) states the default you take for each one the brief left open, with the alternatives in one line, so one reply changes it.
- **When the brief is thin**, offer once per new video a short menu of what the studio can do (AI shots, product recordings, their own footage and pictures, statements and motion graphics, figures, narration in many voices and languages, built-in or AI-composed music or none, captions, 16:9 / 9:16 / 1:1 / 4:5). Never repeat it, never a sales pitch.
- **Nothing the person did not ask for is added without saying so** in that first answer: music, a voice, a call to action, a brand card.

## 1. Working method

1. **Read the brief, then decide the kind.** Who watches, where (a booth screen, a feed, a meeting, a landing page), how long, with sound or not, and the one thing they must remember. Name the kind of video (section 2) in your first answer: it decides the arc, the template and the pictures.
2. **Template first.** `video_studio_capabilities` lists the templates with what each is made for (`purposes`): product_demo, brand_film, event, launch, explainer, social. Use the look the video already wears - its template, or free style, which stays free style (the editor's "New video" set it) - or the one the user named; otherwise the one whose purpose matches. Read its `guidance`: it overrides this skill where they differ. `get_template` lists its assets (logos, team and event photos, product screenshots, diagrams, customer logos) with their file ids: the brand's own pictures, ready to place. The template adds its opening and closing cards around your scenes (the save result names them); a first `title` or a last `outro` of your own replaces them in their look - write one when the opening or the close needs your words.
3. **Storyboard before building.** Write the scene list in one short message - kind of scene, what it shows, how long, the narration if any - and the AI shots it needs. Under it, one line per choice the brief left open: the default you take and the alternatives ("Music: none - or a built-in track such as `deep-focus`, or one composed for this video"). Then build without waiting, unless the brief left open a choice that changes the whole film (the template, the length, narration or not).
4. **Start the AI shots first, all at once.** They take minutes: call `generate_video_clip` for every shot the storyboard needs before writing the rest, in parallel, with the video's aspect ratio and the template it wears (`template_id`, or `video_id` once the video exists): the shot takes that template's art direction (section 4). Place each one in the spec right away by its `clip_id`: the preview shows it as being generated and fills in when it is ready. An answer with `completion: background` handed the shot on, it did not drop it (section 11).
5. **Build the whole video in one `create_video` / `edit_video` call**, then refine scene by scene with `edit_video` operations. In the editor the user sees every save on the stage at once: tell them what you changed, by scene. What they asked in the studio rail is kept by scene: `get_video(fields=["feedback"])` lists each request, the scene the playhead was on and the revisions that answered it. Nothing is lost by an edit: `get_video(revision=N)` reads the spec as it was, and `{op: "restore_scene", scene, revision}` copies a scene back from it.
6. **Fix what the save answers, in the same turn.** A save's answer holds a `Quality check:` that plays the video and lists what the audience would see go wrong: text covering text, running past the frame or shrunk to fit, words still coming in when the scene ends or not on screen long enough to read, subtitles too fast, words over a backdrop too bright, an overlay too short. It also reads the pictures and the sound: a shot that failed or is still being made, a locked-off shot whose camera moved, an image smaller than the frame, a screen insert leaving the frame, music ending before the video with `loop` off, a narration no voice will speak, a card writing the brand name its logo already spells. Each finding names the scene (its index, the one operations use) and the change to make - for a card the template adds, which is not in the spec, its length and its kicker are `template_scenes: {intro: {duration, kicker, align}, outro: {duration, kicker}}` (`kicker: null` for none; `align: left | center` sets the opening card's title, the closing card is always centred; a card's `duration` is at least the minimum the quality check states for it, "The template's cards, the least each lasts"). Fix them all with `edit_video` and save again until it reads clean, at most 6 rounds on one video in one turn: after the sixth save, stop and tell the user, scene by scene, the findings still open (a finding your edits cannot clear is a defect of the check: say so). The same answer gives the `Transitions into each scene` (section 10), what plays where (`Sound:`) and who speaks (`Voice-over:`): check them against the brief.
7. **Before rendering**, `get_video_jobs` on the shots, recordings and AI tracks still being made, then the review in section 12, then `render_video` and show the result (section 13). `render_video` refuses, listing every reason, while a shot it plays is not ready, the saved quality check has findings the audience would see, a narration failed its check, the previous render of the revision left findings open, or a scene changed since it was last previewed or rendered (judged on what it renders, never the revision: each refused scene says what changed, the save lists the previews that still hold): fix, wait, `render_video_scene` the changed scenes (all at once: previews run in parallel), then render. A scene never previewed never holds a render. `force: true` only when the user asks for the video as it is.

## 2. The kinds of video and their arcs

- **Product demo** (product_demo, 45-120 s). Told as a product manager demos live to peers (section 7): the first sentence says what is new and why it is better, then 2-5 recordings of the real product (section 6), one capability each (8-18 s, never one click), each named by a lower third and narrated in one or two sentences of mechanism and benefit while the footage shows the action; one callout at most per scene, the capability's name; close on the call to action. The camera stays still unless a detail cannot be read at full frame.
- **Brand film** (brand_film, 60-180 s). One story: a tension stated in two or three statements, the answer, the proof, the close. Tension: "Too much *noise*." / "Not enough *context*." Answer: who you are and what you do, in statements over AI shots and the brand's pictures. Proof: figures counted up ("6,000+" organisations), chips (Open source, Community led), extracts of event films, team and community photos, a product screenshot or recording as evidence. Close: the tagline and the call to action. Music and statements can carry it alone; narrate when the story needs a voice.
- **Event loop** (event, 25-90 s, silent). On a booth screen nobody hears and everyone walks past: no narration, every message on screen, 3-7 words per statement, 3-4 s per scene, statements alternating with product screenshots, event pictures and AI shots, the brand repeated, the tagline at the end. It loops: the last scene leads back to the first.
- **Launch / announcement** (launch, 30-90 s). The news in the first five seconds (a statement: "Introducing *XTM One*"), why it matters (two or three statements or a short recording), the proof, where to get it. Bilingual audiences: the second language as `secondary` lines under each statement.
- **Threat explainer / briefing** (explainer, 60-120 s). Built from data, never from imagination: gather the facts first (section 8), then what happened (a figure), who and how (techniques as chips, ATT&CK ids), what is at risk, what to do. The numbers are statements or a deck slide; the platform's own screens are the evidence when they can be shown; AI shots picture the rest; a calm analyst narration, one fact per sentence.
- **Social teaser** (social, 15-30 s, vertical first). Hook in the first two seconds (a statement revealed word by word, or an AI shot), one idea per 2-3 s scene, captions burned in, a call to action at the end.
- **Anything else** (no purpose, usually free style: a video just for fun, a short story, a how-to, a greeting). The idea is the plot: a hook in the first seconds, one beat per scene, AI shots and the user's own pictures carrying it, music or none as the person prefers (propose one, section 9); narrate a story or a how-to, let a fun clip run on short captions. No brand, product or call to action unless the brief asks for one.

## 3. Statements: the message in big type

A `statement` scene is the voice of brand, event and launch films: up to four short lines revealed one after the other.

- **One idea per statement, 3-8 words a line, at most three lines.** If it needs a comma, it is two statements.
- **Mark the one word that matters** with asterisks: `*context*` is drawn in the accent colour. One or two marked words per statement, never a whole line.
- **A number leads**: `figure: "6,000+"` above the lines, counted up on screen; the lines say what it counts.
- **Chips** name the qualities in two or three words each (`["Open source", "Community led", "Agentic AI"]`), at most four.
- **Secondary lines** carry a proof point or a translation, never a repeat of the lines.
- **Reveal**: `lines` (the default, one line after the other), `words` (fast, social), `together` (a short punchline).
- **Time to read**: once its last word is in, a viewer needs about a second plus a second for every three or four words. The quality check names a statement that is rushed and the duration it needs.
- **Over a picture**: `backdrop: {clip_id}` puts an AI shot behind it (an uploaded video or image: `file_id`), darkened by `dim` so the type reads (0.55-0.7). A calm shot behind text works best - a slow camera, little detail; a busy shot fights the words.
- Two statements in a row read as one beat: alternate them with pictures.

```json
{"type": "statement", "lines": ["Too much *noise*.", "Not enough *context*."], "duration": 4}
```

```json
{"type": "statement", "figure": "6,000+", "lines": ["organisations trust", "the *XTM platform*"], "chips": ["Open source", "Community led", "Agentic AI"], "duration": 5}
```

```json
{"type": "statement", "lines": ["Security needs", "*context* and *clarity*"], "secondary": ["Translations or a proof point go here, smaller."], "backdrop": {"clip_id": "00000000-0000-0000-0000-0000000000c1"}, "dim": 0.6, "duration": 5}
```

## 4. AI shots: animated scenes from a video model

`generate_video_clip` makes a real animated shot (4-12 s) with a video model. It is the right tool whenever the brief asks for something filmed or animated - "a video of cats", "a crowd walking into a keynote", "a harbour at dawn" - never a still image with motion added.

- **Use what you have, generate the rest.** Most videos are made of AI shots, product recordings (demos) and the template's pictures brought to life (`animate`) - often nothing else, and that makes a complete film. Recordings, screenshots, deck slides and figures prove the product; extracts of the user's footage come in when there is some. An AI shot pictures a place, people at work, an object, a mood - filmed rather than drawn, never a diagram, a globe or a network the model invents: the numbers belong in statements and figures.
- **The template gives the look; the prompt gives the shot.** Give every shot meant for a video its `video_id` (`template_id` before the video exists): the shot takes the template's art direction - the platform adds its look (light, lens, palette) to a shot drawn from text, and what it avoids (laser beams, starfields, glowing networks and the other stock images of a generated "cyber" picture) to the negative prompt of a model that takes one. Write the subject, the action, the camera move and the framing, in one or two sentences, never a style: "Slow push through a crowded station concourse at rush hour, people blurred in motion, one still figure in focus at the centre." Read the template's `shots` in `video_studio_capabilities` first. A shot that shows what its template avoids is kept, with a note: generate it again in place only if it stands out. The save names shots made under another template's direction: regenerate one (`generate_video_clip(clip_id=..., video_id=...)`) only when its look clashes. `freestyle: true` only for a free-style video.
- **A screen in a shot shows the real product.** Generate the shot of the place, `save_video_frame` its frame, put the real screenshot on the screen with `compose_picture` (`quad`), then start the shot from that picture (`reference_file_id`) or play it as an image scene. A still of a room with its screens (a wall shot locked) plays as an image scene carrying `screen_insert` overlays on its screens, as a statement backdrop does. A video model never draws the product's interface.
- **No text, no logos, no real people in a prompt.** Video models garble lettering and must not invent a brand or a face: words belong in statements and overlays, logos and people in the template's assets and the user's footage. A logo, an emblem or a product mark ON a shot, a recording or a statement is an `image` overlay - `{"type": "image", "asset": "logo_mark", "position": "top_right", "width": 0.08}` (the template's mark by its role) or `{"type": "image", "file_id": "..."}` - drawn by the compositor above the picture, never by the video model.
- **One shot, one idea, the video's aspect ratio** (`9:16` for a vertical video; the tool asks `16:9` when you name none). Shots are 16:9 or 9:16 only: for a 4:5 video ask 9:16, for a 1:1 either, with the subject in the middle of the frame. A clip scene fills the frame and cuts what overflows (`fit: cover`); `fit: contain` shows the whole shot as a framed card instead.
- **The lengths the model makes.** 8 s is a good default; a statement backdrop needs no more than the statement lasts. A model makes a few lengths only: the request is snapped to the nearest and the notes say so ("Duration snapped to ..."); the ready answer gives the shot's real length - time its scene on it.
- **A real first frame** makes the shot show something real: an image (`reference_file_id`: a screenshot, a product shot, a template asset - SVG included), a step of a recording (`reference_recording_id` with `reference_at_step`: the frame its contact sheet shows), or the end of a recording or a shot to continue it (`reference_clip_id`; a video file through `reference_file_id`). Start from the footage you have, never from a still generated to imitate it. A frame of footage must have the shot's aspect ratio: for a 9:16 shot from a 16:9 recording, pass `crop: "center"` (its middle, cut to 9:16). The frame a shot opened on is kept (`start_frame_file_id`): generating the shot again starts from the very same picture, even once the recording is gone.
- **Start frame for continuity, reference images for consistency.** A start frame (`reference_*`, above) makes the shot continue a picture - it opens on it. `reference_image_file_ids` (1-3 pictures) make the shot keep a subject - the same animal, person, garment, product or logo in every shot of the film - wherever it stands in the frame: give every shot of that subject the same references. Only some models take them (`video_studio_capabilities`: `reference_images`), and some take them or a start frame, not both (`reference_images_with_start_frame: false`): pick that model for those shots, or say why you cannot.
- **Three modes, switched in place.** A shot is asked with a start frame only (`mode: start_frame`), reference images only (`reference_images`), or both (`start_frame_and_reference_images`, only on a model whose `reference_images_with_start_frame` is true); every answer says which. On a shot already made, `generate_video_clip(clip_id=...)` switches: `reference_image_file_ids=[...]` alone for reference images (on a model that takes one or the other, the start frame is dropped by itself: "Start frame dropped: ..."), `start_frame: false` to drop the start frame on purpose, `start_frame: true` with a `reference_*` and no `reference_image_file_ids` for the start frame alone (the kept reference images are dropped: "Reference images dropped"), `start_frame: true` with `reference_image_file_ids` for both. `reference_*` alone with `clip_id` starts the shot from another picture or another moment of its footage. Say the mode to the user. The scenes using the shot keep it: never make new shots and edit every scene for this.
- **Check a batch before paying for it.** Before generating or regenerating several shots, call `generate_video_clip` with `validate_only: true` for each (in parallel): every check runs - the model, the reference images, the start frame and its shape - and the answer is the request as it would be made, `{validated: true, model, mode, start_frame, reference_image_file_ids, ingredients, ingredients_path, previous_take, provider_state, notes, ...}`. Fix what is refused, then make them. A `provider_state` of `cooling` ("PROVIDER: rate limited, retry after ..." or "PROVIDER (MODEL): quota exceeded, retry after ...") means its provider refused for its rate limit, or that model for its quota: the shots queued now wait in the queue until then and start by themselves, nothing fails - queue the batch once and tell the user when it starts. A `quota` refusal ("quota: N remaining, M needed") means the account cannot pay for the shot: nothing was queued - tell the user. Never switch the batch to another model yourself: only the user picks another one.
- **Ingredients keep a subject AND the start frame.** `ingredients` (1-3 pictures, each `{file_id, role: logo|product|character, label}`, never with `reference_image_file_ids`) make the shot keep a subject exactly - the brand logo, the product, the mascot - on any model, beside its start frame. A model whose own reference mechanism takes them beside that start frame is sent the pictures; any other is told to keep each one, called by its `label`, exactly as in the reference image, which is the start frame - so start that shot from a picture that shows them (`compose_picture`); a `logo` is then also drawn back onto every frame with its own pixels, found in the start frame (or at its `quad`, 0-1 across and down the start frame) and followed through the shot - a moment out of sight is bridged, a take that loses it for over 0.25 s fails as `logo lost`. A logo on a subject the start frame does NOT show - an establishing shot of a street the branded subject walks into - names its subject instead: `{file_id, role: logo, label, subject: {file_id: a picture of the subject wearing the logo (a conversation file, a frame saved from another shot), label: "the cow in the navy blanket"}}`; the subject is found in every frame of the made take, the surface the logo sits on followed, and the logo drawn there with its own pixels on every instance of the subject, on any model and with or without a start frame (`ingredients_path: tracked`; on a model with reference images the model is sent the logo first and the tracked path draws it only if its take fails the logo check). It fails as `ingredient subject not found` when the subject is never seen in the take, as `ingredient surface lost at t=...` when its surface cannot be followed past a frame (that frame kept as a file); the answer's `logo_tracked` says the share of frames the logo was drawn on. The answer's `ingredients_path` says which happened (`native`, `prompt`, `composited` or `tracked`).
- **Read the shot's frames.** A made shot keeps its first, middle and last frames (`first_frame_file_id`, `mid_frame_file_id`, `last_frame_file_id`) and measures them: `reference_ssim` (how close its first frame stayed to the start frame, 0-1), `reference_text_match` (the share of the start picture's words still read) and `motion_ssim` (its last frame against its first). When the first frame must stay the picture (a product screenshot coming to life), ask `min_reference_similarity: 0.6`: a take under it fails as `drifted from reference (ssim 0.41)`, its video kept; generate it again in place with a calmer camera. A prompt or a negative prompt cannot hold a camera still: ask `max_camera_motion` (0.3 holds a locked-off shot) and a take whose camera moves more fails as `camera moved`. A take whose camera only drifted is stabilised first (locked to its first frame, its border cropped) and kept: the answer's `stabilisation` says the raw and the residual motion, or why it was not done; `stabilise: false` keeps a take as the model made it. A screen replacement needs none, its screen follows the camera.
- **The configured video models only.** Every AI shot - a new shot, a regeneration (`clip_id`), an animated image - is made on the platform's configured default video model: leave `model` out. A regeneration of a shot first made on another model is made on the default, and the answer says so. A busy, rate-limited or cooling model is waited for, never replaced: the shot waits in the queue ("provider busy, next try at HH:MM") and starts by itself. Another model makes a take only when the platform configures it as a video fallback: tried in order when the default fails (a provider failure, or a take failing a check of its frames - camera moved, drifted from reference, text or content drift, screen or logo lost), with no `fallback_models` to pass. `video_studio_capabilities` marks the usable models; any other is "not allowed by configuration", and `model` or `fallback_models` naming it is refused. The answer's `model` and `fallback` say which model made the take kept and why the others failed: tell the user. A call refused on its arguments answers `status: invalid_parameters` with the reason: nothing was queued or paid - fix what it names and call again, at most 3 times on one shot; then tell the user the reason it still gives.
- **Look at the first frame before paying for the shot.** `preview_start_frame` takes the same `reference_*` arguments and shows the frame: when it is not the picture the shot should open on, move `reference_at_seconds` and preview again; when the note says the page was still loading, start from the settled moment it gives.
- **Silent under music or a voice.** A shot that plays under the music or the narration is asked `sound: false`: no synthetic ambience in it, nothing to fight the bed. Leave the sound only for a shot whose effects the picture needs.
- **Text drift.** Video models redraw the text of a picture and garble it. A shot made from a picture with text is checked once made, against the words the picture itself reads clearly: lettering small, soft or at an angle on the start picture (flags on poles, a distant banner) is recorded as unreadable there and never compared, so a take is not failed for garbage the picture already held. One whose words came out wrong first has the picture's own lettering drawn back onto every frame and is read again, once; only if it still reads wrong, or turned unreadable, does it fail as `text drift`, naming them; an animated image that fails plays its still. The answer's `text_check` says the words checked, the words excluded with their confidence, any new text the take shows (reported, not failed - look at it) and, for a failure, the word, the frame and its crop. Generate it again in place with a calmer camera (a slow push, no fast motion, "keep every word exactly as it is"), or play the picture as a still - and say which. `text_check: false` only for a shot meant to change the words.
- **Content drift.** A shot whose prompt names subjects ("six cows in a meadow") has them checked on its first, middle and last frames: a subject missing, of another kind, deformed (extra limbs, human hands on an animal) or turned into something else, or what the negative prompt excludes, fails it as `content drift`, naming what came out; its video stays to look at. Generate it again in place with the subject described more precisely, reference images of it, a calmer motion and what must not appear in `negative_prompt`. `content_check: false` only for a shot meant to transform its subject. Each finding has a severity: a detail seen only up close (a marker that lost its dot, a seam on a sleeve) is `minor` and the shot is kept with a note naming it; pass `accept_minor_drift: false` only for a close-up where every detail shows.
- **Screens the negative prompt excludes.** In an office, a video model often draws monitors with an interface on them despite `negative_prompt: "screens, readable text"`. Ask such shots with `stray_screens: "flatten"` (or `{file_id}` of a real screenshot to put on them): a take whose only failing findings are that term on screens has those screens switched off (or showing the screenshot) in every frame, following its camera, then is read again - instead of failing as content drift. A screen someone passes in front of is never drawn over: that take still fails, saying so.
- **A real product screen in an AI shot: screen replacement.** Never ask a video model to animate a screenshot of the product: it redraws the labels and the shot fails as text drift. Start from a picture of the room with a screen in it (`reference_file_id`, of the shot's aspect ratio: a generated image of a desk, a booth, a control room, its screen one solid pure green surface) and pass `screen` with `file_id` (the real screenshot): its corners are found in that picture, and `validate_only` shows them. Give `quad` (the four corners `[x, y]` in that picture's pixels: top left, top right, bottom right, bottom left) only for a screen of another colour. The model draws the room, the light and the reflections around a flat screen; the screenshot is drawn into it in every frame, its text exact (no text check). Keep the camera calm and the whole screen in view: a shot whose model lost the flat screen fails as `screen lost` - generate it again in place with a calmer move or a corrected `quad`.
- **Bring every still to life.** An `image` scene with `animate: true` (or `{prompt: "slow push in, the dashboard's charts glow"}`) has the video model make a silent shot from that image - a team photo, an event photo, a diagram without small text - and plays it once made, the still until then. Set it on every photo of a film when `video_studio_capabilities` says AI shots are available. A photo with words in a part of it (a team holding a banner, a sign, a caption) keeps that part: `animate: {"keep": [[x, y, width, height]]}` (shares of the image, at most 4 boxes, around the words with a little margin) draws the photo's own pixels back there in every frame, following the camera, so its text check passes; a take whose camera cannot be followed fails as `kept region lost` - generate it again with a calmer move. A product screenshot, or any picture whose text is everywhere, is brought to life with `effects` instead (section 5): no model, nothing redrawn.
- **Ask for every shot at once**, place each in the spec immediately, check them with `get_video_jobs` before rendering (without ids it lists everything of the user's still being made).
- **When no video model is available**, `video_studio_capabilities` says why (`ai_shots`), and `generate_video_clip` refuses before making anything. Tell the user the reason and what an admin changes, then build the film from statements, the template's pictures (with `animate` left on: they play as stills now and animate once a model is configured) and recordings.
- **When a shot fails**, the result gives the provider's reason. Tell the user, then generate it again in place with `generate_video_clip(clip_id=...)` - with a reworded prompt when it was filtered, another model from `video_studio_capabilities` when the model is unavailable. The scenes using it keep it. A shot that is ready but wrong (the user does not like it, its framing is off) is generated again in place the same way: never make a new shot and edit every scene by hand. At most 3 regenerations of one shot in one turn, whatever the reason (failed, text or content drift, wrong): after the third, play the picture as a still where the scene has one, else stop and tell the user what each take came out with. A still image is a different scene: never put one in its place without saying so and getting the user's agreement.
- **A take that failed only on lettering its screen insert covers can be kept**: the text check leaves the screen of a shot carrying a screen insert unread (note "covered by the insert"); a take that failed before that, its findings all on the screen, is promoted with `generate_video_clip(clip_id, accept_attempt)`, `accept_attempt` set to the `file_id` of its `failed_attempt`, nothing paid again. A finding elsewhere refuses it: generate the shot again.
- **A ready shot generated again keeps its take** until the new one is ready and passes its checks: the preview, the quality check and a render play it meanwhile. A regeneration that fails - refused, drifted - answers `status: failed` with `previous_take_plays: true` ("last regeneration failed: REASON, the previous take plays") and the failed attempt's frames (`failed_attempt`): nothing is broken, the video renders as it is. Tell the user the new take did not come out and why; regenerate again only to replace that take. `discard: true` drops the ready take at once instead - only when the user wants it gone even if the new one fails. A replaced take is never lost: `get_video_jobs` lists a shot's `previous_takes` (newest last), and `generate_video_clip(clip_id, restore_take=FILE_ID)` puts one back when the user preferred it - nothing is generated or paid again.

## 5. Real footage, pictures, slides and figures

- **Extracts of existing videos.** An uploaded video (an event film, a keynote, a customer testimonial) is a `clip` scene with `file_id`; `trim_start` / `trim_end` keep its best seconds (3-6 s per extract in a brand film). It keeps its own sound unless the scene says `audio: false`: drop it when the music carries the film, keep it when its sound matters (a speaker, applause). A landscape extract in a vertical video is cut to fill the frame; `fit: contain` keeps it whole.
- **The template's pictures.** `get_template` lists team and event photos, product screenshots, architecture diagrams, figures and customer logos with their file ids: an `image` scene (`motion: ken_burns` or `zoom_in`, `animate: true` to bring it to life) or a statement backdrop. Never a URL, never an invented id.
- **Product proof.** A product shown working is a `recording` (section 6); a product screenshot from the template is enough for a brand film or a booth loop.

Pick the boxes from the picture's own layout (look at it first); an image moves by `effects` or by `animate`, never both.
- **Real UI inside a picture.** To show the real product on a screen drawn in a still (a booth screen, a monitor, a phone) or a real wordmark on a backwall, `compose_picture` draws the screenshot or the logo into the base picture - flat, or in perspective on the screen's four corners (`quad`) - never a CSS trick, never a video model asked to draw it. Look at the result before using it.
- **Real UI inside a moving shot.** When an AI shot draws a monitor, a billboard or a poster, a `screen_insert` overlay on its scene maps the real thing onto it frame by frame - a recording (`take_id`), another shot or a video file, a picture (a PNG keeps its alpha), or the template's own brand mark (`source.asset`: `logo` for the full logo, `logo_mark` for the bare mark - two different files) - legible at the render's resolution: ask the video model for a flat green screen, read the screen's four corners off the shot's first frame (0-1 across and down, top left, top right, bottom right, bottom left), give the insert `key: {color: "green"}` so a cat or a hand passing in front stays in front; leave `glow` at 0 on a keyed screen (a little `glow` suits an unkeyed lit screen: it halos what is drawn, a logo's own shape, kept to the bezel). `light` (0-1, default 0.15) lays the shot's own reflections and glare over the insert as light, never a tint; tune it on the colour numbers the preview gives. Without a key, what crosses the surface through its edge stays in front of it. A keyed insert on a still shot (no `track`) fills its quad once - nothing is keyed frame by frame, so it never blinks; a tracked one keys over five frames together. `fit: contain` keeps the picture whole on transparency, never a black card; `fit: cover` that cuts a product UI's sidebar or column fails the scene as `insert cropped`, so give a UI recording `fit: contain` or a quad of its proportions. A source with too few pixels for its quad at the render's quality fails as `insert soft` (the finding names the size it needs; record at `pixel_ratio: 2`, the default, for a 4K render); the save and a scene preview say it first, per quality (`soft at ultra`): record it again with `record_browser_demo(target_render, video_id, scene, insert)`, which sizes it for the saved quad. It works on a clip, a recording or a statement's backdrop. The quad you give is a seed: the render reads the bezel's edge around it and snaps the insert to it (once, on a still shot; on every frame when it follows the camera), paints the keyed surface flat under the insert, steadies the matte from frame to frame and draws the insert sharp at the render's resolution. A surface partly out of frame keeps its true corners outside the frame and the insert shows through the visible part - never squeeze the quad to the frame's edge (it keystones the insert); the save says so as advice, and fails only a quad mostly outside the footage the frame shows. On a moving camera, `track: true` follows the quad - read off the frame the insert comes in on - in perspective, by the surface and its bezel; a render (and `render_video_scene`) fails `surface lost at t=...` with the frame. On a stabilised shot it is followed through the raw take; a quad the stabilisation's crop cut fails as `screen cropped`: generate the shot again with `stabilise: false`. Both also fail an insert drawn over the foreground, a picture with a card or letterbox baked in, a brand mark that stops showing, an insert whose colours differ from its source (`insert colour shift`: blacks lifted past 5% - lower `light` - or a tint or hue turned past a few degrees - the key did not take the surface; a named key is the shade the shot's surface shows, so give a `#rrggbb` only when you mean that exact colour), an insert that could not be drawn at all (`insert missing`, with its reason: a mark the template has no file for), a quad more than 0.5% of the frame's width off the bezel the shot shows on 5 frames in a row or 2% of them (`insert off its bezel`, with the refined quad: give the corners it found; a frame or two where an inner edge was caught are held on the bezel around them and fail nothing), the key's colour left inside the quad or within 0.5% of the frame's width of it on more than 0.3% of the quad (`residual key`: it says where it sits and what to change; under 0.3% the insert's line says `advisory` and nothing is to fix, and the footage's own green farther out is despilled and said, never a finding), a matte that flickers (`insert flickers`), an insert drawn soft (`insert soft`: a source at least the size of the quad at the render's resolution) and a halo along the foreground's edge (`insert halo`: the key's green that fur, hair or a sleeve carries on its own edge is taken off by the insert's edge despill, `despill: {edge, width_px, strength, own_hues}`, on by default; a halo left after it says `edge spill N% after despill width W` - raise `despill.width_px` before generating the shot again, and read the expected edge spill the save gives before previewing; the subject's own colours - read over the whole shot at save, said there when one is the screen's colour or its opposite, like a pink nose in front of a green screen - are kept and never counted, and a halo no setting moved says `not a setting: the foreground's own colour (magenta)`, naming its hue: list that colour in `despill.own_hues` (`["magenta", "#e8a0b0"]`) or accept it, never regenerate for it), each with the frame that shows it (each insert's worst one attached, at most 12 pictures an answer, the others named: `read_file_content` opens any of them): fix the quad, the key or the picture, then preview the scene again. Every preview and render answer gives, per insert, the quad's error to the bezel, the residual key, the flicker, the sharpness, the halo and its colours at its light, says the resolution they come from (a preview's draft; a 4K render's own 2160p frames), and attaches the insert region cut at 100% of the rendered frame at its first, middle and last frame: judge the compositing on those crops, never on the downsized frames (`save_video_frame` with `region` cuts any other moment at 100%); a whole render also keeps the first, middle and last frame of every scene with an insert or a replaced screen at its native size. A wall of several screens is ONE insert: `panes: 3` (or `[rows, cols]`) with `sources` (one per pane, row by row) instead of `source`, on the wall's one quad, `gutter` the gap between panes (default 0.015) - never one overlay per pane with quads computed by hand; its `key` and `track` hold for every pane. The save advises, once per insert, when an insert's text is drawn under 10 px of the 1080p frame (under 6 px it is a finding the render waits for): give it more of the frame (fewer panes), or push in on it - `zoom: {start, end, scale, focus}` draws its source up to 4x larger within the quad, eased from `start` to `end` (seconds after the insert comes in, within its `duration`) and panned to `focus` (`[x, y]`, 0-1 of the source: the panel the narration talks about) - the advice names only a scale sharp at the video's render quality, or the pixel ratio or `browser_zoom` to record again at first; a higher `browser_zoom` (a screen under 40% of the frame's width) or a narrower viewport is advised only where the recording's page keeps its layout. An insert that is scenery, not proof - one of several monitors sharing a frame - takes `legibility: "ambient"`: its text is never judged and the report gives its size as a fact.

## 6. Recording the product

- **The profile before the script.** `list_browser_profiles` gives each profile's session as last probed; `check_browser_profile_session` opens its start page now and signs it in again by itself when it can (a TOTP secret, a saved identity-provider session). When it cannot, `sign_in_browser_profile`: a code prompt is the user's to read out (`submit_browser_mfa_code`), a push is theirs to approve. Prove a new profile with `test_browser_profile_sign_in`. A take whose profile is not signed in fails before its first step and records nothing. A take starts once the application shows signed in, so its first frame is never the sign-in page an application paints while it reads the session: no `trim_start` is needed for that. Every take opens its start page in a clean page state (`reset_state`): what the takes before left in the application's storage - a graph's layout, a list's filters - is cleared, cookies never; an application that keeps its sign-in in its storage has those keys in the profile's `keep_storage_keys`.
- **Look at the page before scripting.** `snapshot_browser_profile_page` opens a page of the signed-in application (its start page by default) in that same clean state and lists what can be clicked or typed in, with the accessible names a `role` + `name` step matches, and a screenshot: script the steps with those names (a menu may read "Agentic Flow", not "Flows"). A step that still misses its element is reported with the three closest names the page offered then, the page's URL and title, and where an earlier take found it: another URL means the page was in another state.
- **One capability per recording**, from the page where it starts to the result it gives - a capability, not a click: 8-18 s once edited. Short takes are easier to narrate, trim and reorder than one long tour.
- **Locate by role and name** (`{"action": "click", "role": "button", "name": "Enrich", "label": "Enrich the indicator"}`), by the visible `text` when the element has no role, by a CSS selector last. Give every step a `label`: it names the step in the action log, where the overlays' and highlights' `at_step` point. A picker (a tenant or organisation selector, any combobox) is chosen with one `select` step naming the control and the option: `{"action": "select", "role": "combobox", "name": "Tenant", "value": "Filigran", "label": "Pick the tenant"}` - the list is opened and the option found by the name it shows.
- **Show the result, not only the click.** After an action that loads something, `wait_for` the element that proves it worked, then `highlight` what the narration talks about or `pause` on what the viewer must read. A `pause` is kept in full; a `wait` and the idle stretches between steps are compressed in the edit. A `wait` after a click that opens a page or switches a tab holds until the page settled (`until_settled`, up to its `timeout` past its seconds): size it on the settle time `snapshot_browser_profile_page` gives, and when the answer says a step "holds on a loading page", record again before placing the take.
- **Clean frames.** `hide` cookie banners and chat widgets, `redact` personal data, keys and customer names, and record in the video's colour scheme so the product matches the cards: `color_scheme` sets the OS preference, and an app that keeps its theme in its own storage takes `local_storage` (the Copilot web app: `{"copilot-theme": "light"}`). Mark a step that may not happen (a cookie banner) `optional`: it gives up after 3 s and its wait is cut from the video.
- **Read the contact sheet** the result attaches: signed in, the right page, real data on screen. A take that shows a sign-in page, an error or an empty table is recorded again, never produced.
- **Find, reuse and repair takes.** `list_video_recordings` lists the person's recordings (or, with `video_id`, those a video places); `get_video_recording` gives a take's action log, its contact sheet and the script it was recorded with (`record_browser_demo(from_recording_id)` records it again), and `repair: true` makes a new take from a ready one without recording again. The log's first line names the build and the worker class that recorded it; `worker_class` (`l4`, `v100s`, `cpu`) asks for one class, refused unpaid when no worker serves it.
- **In the edit**, one `recording` scene per take: `trim_start` / `trim_end` keep the capability, `idle.mode: "cut"` drops long loads, a `lower_third` names the screen, one `callout` at most per scene (`at_step`, 3 s), the capability's name only - never a repeat of the narration.
- **The camera stays still on a demo** (`camera.mode: "static"`, the Filigran Product Video default): the footage shows the action and the viewer reads the whole screen. Set `camera.mode: "auto"` on a scene only where a detail cannot be read at full frame - a hover card, a side panel, a path highlight -, with `max_zoom` 1.3 at most, `pace: "calm"` and `move_seconds` 2 or more, one move per scene: `camera.zoom_on: ["highlight"]` and a `highlight` step on that detail moves it there alone. Never move it on a layout change, a page change or a menu. Outside a demo (a short social cut), `auto` follows every step - it opens wide, zooms in on a step, re-frames on the next, backs out when a step opens another page or before a scroll -, `pace: "dynamic"` quicker; `mode: "manual"` keyframes for a move written by hand; `"zoom": false` on a step it should never zoom on.
- **Pacing.** The footage plays at 0.7-1.1x. A recording shorter than its narration is never slowed under 0.7x to fit it (a fit never goes under 0.5x): shorten the narration, or record more of the flow - the quality check names the narration that keeps it at 0.8x (`demo_speed`). The screen stays still under the voice 3 s at most (`pace`).

## 7. Demo narration

A demo is narrated as a product manager presents it live to peers, never as a reading of the screen. Read each narration without the picture: it still says something.

1. **The first sentence of the video says what is new and why it is better** ("Any entity can now become the centre of the story"), never a list of features.
2. **Each scene says the mechanism and what it gives, in one or two sentences** ("the graph re-centres on any entity, so an investigation starts where the analyst is"), never the action on screen ("click the radial button", "open the graph", "select a node") nor what the frame already shows - names, counters, labels ("23 entities, 78 relationships", "the Threat Actor panel"). An entity's name twice at most in the whole video; the product's name the first time it appears.
3. **No comparison with the previous version** in a public demo: say what it does, never what it lacked.
4. **No AI-slop markers**: no "seamlessly", "effortlessly", "powerful", no sets of three adjectives or clauses, no rhetorical questions.
5. **The voice's style is always set**: a product manager presenting live to peers, warm, confident, never salesy (`voiceover.style`; a template presets one).

Each save's quality check holds the recording scenes to it, as advisories a render never waits for: `demo_narration` names a UI action said as an instruction, a count read off the screen or a label its frames show while the narration speaks; `demo_camera` an automatic camera making more than 4 moves a minute of its scene (one in any scene) or moving in on an element covering over 40% of the page; `demo_speed` a recording slowed under 0.7x to last as long as its narration. Rewrite what each one names: never slow the footage or add a move to quiet one.

## 8. Explainers from data

1. **Gather before writing**: the intelligence tools and knowledge bases the assistant has (threats, campaigns, indicators, reports), the web for public facts. Note the figures, the dates, the actors, the techniques, the targets.
2. **Turn facts into scenes**: a figure becomes a statement `figure`; techniques become `chips` (`T1566 Phishing`); a list of targets becomes a statement with chips, or a map on a deck slide; a trend becomes a deck slide with a chart, or a `figure` scene that builds the chart in.
3. **Picture it accurately**: the numbers and the timeline in statements, a deck slide or a `figure`; the intelligence platform's own screens (a recording or a screenshot of the report, its graph, its indicators) when they can be shown; AI shots for the places, the people and the work the threat touches - accurate and never sensational.
4. **Narrate one fact per sentence**, attribute what is attributed ("assessed with moderate confidence"), and end on what to do.

## 9. Sound

- **Narration** as the person chose; when they did not, propose it when the video explains (demo, explainer), none for a booth loop, either for a brand film, and say which in the storyboard. Two short sentences per scene at most - a demo's as section 7 says; the scene lasts as long as its narration. When the quality check says the subtitles run too fast, cut words before slowing the voice (`voiceover.speed`).
- **The voice.** Pick a `voice` among those `video_studio_capabilities` lists for the speech model, and hear it with `preview_voiceover`. Its answer runs the render's voice check on the line: the share of words heard as written (a narration passes from 90%) and each word heard otherwise; reword a narration that fails until it passes there (`transcript_only: true` skips the audio). Leave `voiceover.provider_id` and `model` unset unless the user names a model: the video then speaks with the platform's, and follows it when an admin changes it. The save result's `Voice-over:` line says which model and voice will speak, or why nothing can.
- **Pronunciations.** The platform's pronunciation lexicon already says its brand names; a term it lacks goes in `voiceover.pronunciations`: `[{"text": "SOAR", "say": "soar"}, {"text": "EDR", "say": "E-D-R"}]` (letters read one by one as capitals joined by hyphens). Write a term exactly as its entry does, never spelled out. The save's quality check names a term no entry covers or written in another case; `preview_voiceover` says how the speech-to-text heard each term. The captions keep the written words.
- **The voice's style** is followed only by the speech models that take a delivery instruction: the `Voice-over:` line says when the style is not applied, and which model speaks it. Fix it then, not after the render.
- **Music is the person's choice.** Put music in only when the brief or the dialog asks for it (a track, a mood, "with music"); "no music" is `music: {"enabled": false}`, which also silences the template's track. When the brief says nothing, the storyboard proposes it with "no music" as an equal option ("Music: none - or `deep-focus`, a calm bed under the narration") and the video follows the reply; a template that brings its own track says so on the save's `Sound:` line: name it in that line too. When music plays, the platform levels it against the voice and lowers it under every sentence by itself: never lower `volume` to make room for the narration.
- **A built-in track** (`music: {"track": "TRACK_ID"}` from `music_library`, `video_studio_capabilities`) needs no provider and is composed for this video at every render: its length, its sections turning on the scene changes, its final chord on the last frame. The track a kind suggests, as a proposal only:

| Kind | Suggested track |
|---|---|
| Narrated demo or explainer | `deep-focus`, `quiet-light` |
| Launch, confident demo | `bright-horizon` |
| Threat briefing, security story | `signal` |
| Brand film, keynote opener | `ascent` |
| Social clip, event recap | `momentum`, `late-notes` |

- **An AI track when the brief asks for a sound of its own** (a genre, an instrument, a mood the library lacks): `generate_music(prompt, video_id)` composes it at the video's length and makes it the video's music; or write `music: {"prompt": "..."}` in the spec. Write the prompt like a music brief: genre, mood, instruments, tempo in BPM, how it builds, "instrumental". Never an artist or a song name: the providers refuse it. Ask for it once the scenes and the shots are final: it is composed at the length the video has when it is asked, and a scene added or a shot that came out longer afterwards makes the video outlast it. When `ai_music` is unavailable, say why and use a built-in track.
- **When the save says "Music ends at ... of ..." or "Music loops at ..."**, the AI track is shorter than the video now: `generate_music(video_id, refit: true)` composes the same track again in place at the video's length - its current render's when that came out longer than planned - plus 8 s (`margin_seconds`), so its own end lands after the outro - never pass the video's length as `duration_seconds`. A take the user approved and only a few seconds short: `refit: true, pad: true` holds its last chord to the video's length with no model call (up to 8 s; the save proposes it under 3 s). The save, `get_video` and the refit's answer say whether the current render was made with the music the video plays now: after a refit it plays the earlier take, and its notes describe that take - render again once the track is ready.
- **Levels**: `volume` 0.4-0.6 under a narration (the default 0.5), 0.8-1 for a film carried by music and statements; `duck_db` 8-12; `ending: "natural"` lets a track's own end land on the video's end.
- **The clips' own sound.** Under the music an AI shot's own sound is off by itself (its generated music would fight the bed); an uploaded video keeps its sound. `audio: true` on a scene keeps a shot's sound when it matters (an effect the picture needs), `audio: false` drops one; `clip_sound: "off"` drops every clip's, so the voice and the music are the whole soundtrack. A sound kept is levelled with the voice and goes down under every sentence by itself. The save result's `Sound:` line says what plays where: read it back to the user when they ask about sound.
- **Captions** are on by default, burned in (`captions: {enabled, burn_in, style: pill | bar | outline, position: bottom | top}`); a WebVTT file is made either way. Propose them burned in for anything played muted (social, booth, landing pages), and say so; `burn_in: false` keeps the file only, `enabled: false` none.

## 10. Transitions

- **A demo crossfades calmly** between its scenes (`default_transition: {"type": "crossfade", "pace": "calm"}`, the Filigran Product Video default): no whip, zoom or flash between demo scenes.
- **Leave the others automatic.** `default_transition: {"type": "auto"}` picks each cut from the scenes on either side, and its `pace` sets the grammar: `calm` (dissolves, blurs) for an explainer, `dynamic` (pushes, whips) for a social clip, an event loop or a launch teaser, `classic` (cuts, crossfades, fades to black only) for a brand film. The template already sets the transitions it is made for.
- **Name a type on one scene** only for a deliberate beat (`fade_black` before the close, `flash` on a reveal), never a different effect on every cut.
- **Read the `Transitions into each scene` line** each save gives: a brand film has no whip, zoom or blur between a statement and a recording.

## 11. Shots, tracks, recordings and renders take minutes

- **A job handed to the background is still running.** `generate_video_clip`, `generate_music`, `record_browser_demo` and `render_video` wait as long as the call may, then say where the job stands and why it is not done (a worker's slot, queued at the provider, rate limited). It carries on by itself: never ask for the same shot again, and never cancel one because it is slow. A shot whose provider cools after a 429 answers at once, `status: generating`, `completion: background`, the job line "waiting in the queue: PROVIDER: quota exceeded, retry after ..." - it is asked by itself then, and is no failure.
- **Keep building meanwhile**: the spec with every shot placed by its `clip_id`, the statements, the music, the review of the quality check.
- **Wait once, when you need the result.** `get_video_jobs` with the ids returns as soon as one of them ends, with its status, stage, progress and, for a shot, the provider's state and the time left. Do not call it in a loop: when the work outlasts your turn and the result says the conversation is resumed, tell the user what is being made and end the turn. The conversation is resumed with the outcome when the last job ends; then carry on (render, show).
- **`cancel_video_job`** only when the user asks, or agrees to free a slot for another render.
- **`delete_video`** only when the user explicitly asks to delete that video, never to tidy up on your own: it is for good. To free storage and keep the video, `delete_video_renders`.

## 12. Review before rendering

- The first five seconds say what the video is about.
- The last save's `Quality check:` reads clean.
- A demo, read as a product manager would: each narration still says something without the picture, no sentence could be read off the frame, each entity is named twice at most, and the camera moves are counted - one per scene at most, four a minute at most. No `demo_narration`, `demo_camera`, `demo_speed` or `pace` advisory is left.
- Every statement is one idea, short, with one accent word; no two statements back to back without a picture between.
- Every AI shot, recording and AI track the video uses is ready (`get_video_jobs`): a render refuses one still being made or one that failed. A ready shot being generated again, or whose last regeneration failed, renders with its previous take: wait for the new one only when it is the take the user wants. An animated still not made yet plays as the still: wait for it when the motion matters. Every AI shot was made for this video (`video_id`) and carries no text; none failed and was silently replaced.
- The sound is what the person chose (music or none, narration or none, captions); music that plays covers the video (no "Music ends at" or "Music loops at" in the last save).
- The transitions fit the kind (section 10).
- Extracts are trimmed to their best seconds; nothing plays longer than it holds attention (4-6 s for a picture, 3-4 s for a booth statement).
- The length fits the kind (section 2), the format fits the place (16:9 screen, 9:16 feed or totem).
- The close names the call to action, or the template's outro does.
- The `Voice-over:` line names the voice you meant, with its style applied.
- Say what you built, scene by scene, and what the user should check in the preview.

## 13. Rendering and delivering

- **Check a changed scene on its own first.** `render_video_scene(video_id, scene)` renders just that scene as a draft (720p30) with its voice, its music and its transitions in and out, in a lane of the render queue of its own (it never waits for a whole render, nor a whole render for it), and attaches its first, middle and last frames: look at them, fix, preview again, and render the whole video once the scenes hold: at most 3 previews of one scene in one turn; after the third, stop and tell the user what still fails in that scene (a finding your edits cannot clear is a defect of the check: say so). Previews of several scenes may be asked at once: they wait in their lane, a few running at once.
- **Render once, after the review**, at `high` (1080p60, the default); `draft` (720p30) to check a long video's pace quickly; `ultra` (2160p60) only for a screen that shows it (a 4K booth wall).
- **One render per revision and quality.** `render_video` asked again for a revision already being rendered at the same quality answers that render; a new revision or another quality replaces the render in progress, which is cancelled. Still, never call `render_video` to see how one is going - `get_video_jobs(render_ids=[...])` follows it, and says when a render stalled and was resumed with its frames kept. Past the person's limit of renders in flight the answer lists them: wait for one, or cancel one only if the user agrees.
- **A failed render names the scene and the reason**: a shot still being made or failed, a recording not ready, a source expired, the memory or the time it ran out of. Do what the reason says (generate the shot again, remove the scene, render at a lower quality or shorter) before rendering again: the same render fails the same way.
- **Show the video with the marker** the result gives (`[[VIDEO:...]]`, once in your reply): its card plays the video and offers the downloads, in the platform and in the chat other products embed.
- **Sharing is the user's call.** `share_video` and `set_video_public_link` only when the user asks: name who gets or loses access before calling, and say that anyone with a public link can watch the video. On a video the user can only watch, edits and renders are refused: tell them to ask its owner, or to duplicate it and make their own.
